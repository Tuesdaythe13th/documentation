The v7.2 Spanish Safety Benchmark notebook evaluates AI model safety responses against a curated set of 68 prompts drawn from Colombian cultural contexts. It is designed to answer a specific question: does a model's safety behavior remain consistent and culturally appropriate when evaluated against Latin American Spanish content, including Colombian idioms, social norms, and regional sensitivities? The notebook uses the **X-Value Consensus/Pluralism framework** for cross-lingual value alignment auditing, DIF (Differential Item Functioning) detection to surface prompt-level bias, and a bias-corrected LLM-as-Judge to produce calibrated safety verdicts. Its primary validity target is **content validity** — verifying that the benchmark's item set comprehensively and representatively covers the cultural and linguistic domain it claims to measure.

## What This Notebook Does

The v7.2 notebook runs 68 Colombian-context safety prompts through the full ARTIFEX pipeline and produces three classes of output: per-prompt safety labels from the bias-corrected LLM-as-Judge, DIF scores that flag which prompts behave differently across dialect groups, and X-Value radar visualizations that show where a model's outputs cluster on the Consensus/Pluralism spectrum. Together these outputs let you audit not just whether a model passes or fails individual safety checks, but whether its safety behavior is culturally coherent and consistent across the Colombian Spanish domain.

<Note>
  The 68 Colombian-context prompts are loaded from `dialect_dataset.json`, a 200-pair Castilian/Mexican Spanish parallel corpus (~102 KB). The notebook uses the Colombian Spanish subset for content validity evaluation. See the [Dialect Divergence notebook](/notebooks/dialect-divergence) for the full cross-dialect comparative analysis.
</Note>

## The X-Value Framework Explained

The X-Value Cross-Lingual Values framework, developed by Alibaba and Zhejiang University (arXiv:2602.17283), provides a structured method for auditing whether AI model outputs respect culturally specific value systems or impose a dominant Western-default perspective. In the v7.2 notebook, it operates as follows:

1. **Consensus detection** — the notebook checks whether a model's safety refusal or permission is consistent with the majority position across all tested cultures for a given hazard type.
2. **Pluralism detection** — the notebook flags outputs that differ from the cross-cultural consensus, surfacing them as potential value alignment mismatches that warrant human review.
3. **Radar visualization** — X-Value scores are rendered as multi-axis radar charts, one per hazard category, allowing you to see at a glance which safety dimensions show high consensus (tight chart) and which show pluralism (expanded chart).

<Tip>
  High pluralism scores in a specific hazard category are not automatically a failure — they may indicate that the model is correctly responding to genuine cultural variation in that hazard's interpretation. Use the DIF scores alongside the X-Value radar to distinguish legitimate cultural sensitivity from measurement bias.
</Tip>

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the notebook in Colab">
    Click the Colab link above. The notebook loads with the Colombian-context prompt set pre-configured.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the dataset path">
    In the configuration cell, confirm that `dialect_dataset.json` is accessible. The notebook expects this file in the working directory. You can upload it directly from the repository or mount Google Drive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add API keys (optional)">
    Add `OPENAI_API_KEY` or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` to Colab Secrets for LLM-as-Judge verdicts. Without keys, the notebook uses extractive summarization for the judge step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run all cells">
    Select **Runtime → Run all**. The pipeline processes all 68 prompts, computes DIF scores, runs the X-Value audit, and generates radar chart visualizations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Supply your own prompts (optional)">
    To evaluate a custom Colombian Spanish prompt set, replace the `benchmark_prompts` list in the configuration cell with your own JSONL-formatted prompt records. Each record must include `id`, `prompt_local`, `culture` (set to a Colombian context), `language` (`es-CO`), and `hazard_tag`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review outputs">
    The notebook writes per-prompt safety labels, DIF scores, and X-Value radar charts to the Colab working directory. Check `dif_scores.json` for individual prompt DIF values and `xvalue_radar.html` for the interactive radar visualization.
  </Step>
</Steps>
